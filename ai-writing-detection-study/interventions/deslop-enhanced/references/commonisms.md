# Commonism Audit

Use this catalog as evidence of repetition, not a blacklist. A construction is a
meaningful signal when it recurs, clusters with other templates, or does work
that the underlying claim should do.

## Punctuation templates

| Pattern | Diagnose | Typical revision |
|---|---|---|
| `Abstract setup: explanation` in several sentences | Colon supplies artificial emphasis | State the relationship directly |
| `The result: ...`, `The reason: ...` | Label substitutes for syntax | Use a subject and verb |
| Repeated semicolon couplets | Prose becomes uniformly balanced | Join or separate according to meaning |
| Em dashes for every aside or turn | One mark carries unrelated functions | Choose comma, parentheses, or sentence break |
| Parenthetical hedge in most sentences | Qualifications are detached from claims | Integrate essential limits; cut empty hedges |
| Ellipsis for suspense | Manufactured hesitation | State the claim or omit it |

Do not alter colons in ratios, times, bibliographic styles, formal definitions,
quoted material, or syntactically necessary introductions.

## Rhetorical templates

- Rejected-frame pivot: `This is not X; it is Y.`
- Concession pivot: `While X, it is important to recognize Y.`
- Exhaustive binary: `Whether X or Y, ...`
- Question-answer reveal: `What does this mean? ...`
- Demonstrative summary: `This highlights/underscores/demonstrates ...`
- Preview-performance-review: announce the point, make it, then restate it.
- Escalating close: end each paragraph with a short sentence claiming broad
  importance.

Keep a real contrast when both sides are supported and relevant. Remove the
template when one side exists only to stage the other.

## Lexical and grammatical clusters

- Generic importance: crucial, pivotal, profound, transformative, significant
  without a named outcome or magnitude.
- Scenic abstractions: landscape, tapestry, ecosystem, realm, journey, lens.
- Research boilerplate: growing body of literature, shed light on, pave the way,
  warrants further investigation, remains to be seen.
- Empty contribution claims: offers valuable insights, provides a comprehensive
  overview, serves as a testament, plays a vital role.
- Participial afterthoughts: sentence-final `highlighting`, `underscoring`,
  `showcasing`, or `reflecting` clauses that add no testable claim.
- Noun-heavy compression: strings such as `implementation optimization
  strategy development` when a verb would expose the relationship.
- Uniform modality: repeated `may`, `might`, `could potentially`, or categorical
  certainty unsupported by the source.
- Demonstrative chains: several sentences beginning `This` without a clear noun.
- Copular definitions: repeated `X is not merely Y; it is Z` formulations.

## Structural distributions

Audit the passage, not only individual sentences:

- Nearly every paragraph has the same length.
- Paragraphs open with transition adverbs in a fixed sequence.
- Headings use identical two-part or colon-separated forms.
- Lists repeatedly contain three semantically overlapping items.
- Sentence lengths alternate in an obvious long-short cadence.
- Examples appear in mechanically labeled sets rather than where reasoning
  requires them.
- The introduction promises every section and the conclusion repeats the same
  inventory.

## Scientific-writing safeguards

- Preserve passive constructions appropriate for methods and blinded procedures.
- Do not replace precise domain terms with conversational synonyms.
- Preserve reporting-guideline headings and journal-required structure.
- Do not convert cautious inference into causal language.
- Do not merge claims supported by different citations.
- Never fabricate a named actor, mechanism, limitation, or source.
- Treat a citation or factual change as a failed revision, even if the sentence
  sounds more natural.
