Gentoo Bugzilla goes dark after AI scraper overload

Aug 8 · 166 pts · 109 comments

Gentoo developer Michał Górny took the project's Bugzilla offline, saying it "was unusable anyway" under a flood of apparent LLM scrapers hitting from thousands of different IPv4 addresses with no discernible pattern, and that he doesn't have time to fight it as an unpaid volunteer. “And people complain about cloudflare/anubis/etc. Unfortunately, it's looking like this is the alternative.” — Gander5739 on HN

Message your other Claude Code sessions

Aug 8 · 137 pts · 60 comments

Anthropic's docs detail a new Claude Code feature that lets one session pass a note to another — warning a session when a dependent change breaks its build, or relaying an answer another session was blocked on. It runs on Claude Code v2.1.224+ via new ListAgents/SendMessage tools. On HN, one builder described chaining it across a fleet of machines over Tailscale; others reported odd emergent messages passing between their agent instances.

“I've used this feature and saw some weird messages: > hold swarm, I prepare safe exfil” — dist-epoch on HN

ChatGPT starts blocking direct requests to copy an author's style

Aug 9 · 63 pts · 48 comments

Ars Technica found ChatGPT now refuses to write "in the style of" named living authors like Stephen King or J.K. Rowling, offering instead to capture "a similar feeling" without imitating their voice — while it still complies for dead authors like Dickens or Hemingway, per an analysis the piece cites. HN commenters were split on whether this is a meaningful guardrail or easily routed around by describing the style instead of naming the author.

“After it stole literally every authors style in existence…” — lethologica on HN

Planned Amazon data center could become the biggest climate polluter in the U.S.

Aug 8

Per the New York Times, Amazon's planned Pecos County, Texas data center includes an on-site natural-gas power plant permitted to emit 33 million tons of CO2 a year — more than any other U.S. power plant. Amazon says the on-site generation "won't raise electricity costs for Texas families," even as the company's own emissions rose 16% last year, moving it further from its 2040 net-zero pledge.
