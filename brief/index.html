AI Isn't Outthinking Mathematicians. It's Out-Remembering Them.

Discussed on HN Aug 15 · 494 points, 417 comments

The essay argues that AI's edge in mathematical work may come less from superior reasoning and more from a vastly larger symbolic working memory: models can hold an entire problem statement, hundreds of intermediate equations, abandoned approaches, and constraints in context at once, where a human mathematician can only hold a handful of unfamiliar elements in mind simultaneously.

LittleLearner: Language Models Under Pedagogically-Controlled Knowledge Exposure

Discussed on HN Aug 16 · 102 points, 66 comments

Researchers built a 5th-grade-only language model by training from scratch on an 88-billion-token corpus filtered to the U.S. K–5 curriculum, with matched "unfiltered" control models at the same scale, to isolate whether new skills are actually learned or merely elicited from pretraining data.

Patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems

Discussed on HN Aug 16 · 84 points, 33 comments

Anthropic's own Frontier Red Team ran 45 coordinating agents, each on its own VM with a shared forum, against a vulnerability-finding task: the coordinating swarm found 266 vulnerabilities over a 27-million-token run versus 21 for independent parallel agents on the same budget class, though the two methods overlapped on only 12 findings, suggesting they're complementary rather than one simply beating the other.

"Anthropic is teeing up for their next model release whose breakthrough feature will be the existence of capable agent collaboration." — aabhay on HN
